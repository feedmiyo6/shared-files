Kimchi Jjigae (김치찌개)

45 min · 2 servings
Ingredients: 150g pork belly · 1½ cups aged kimchi + 3 tbsp kimchi brine · 200g firm tofu · ½ onion · 3 garlic · 2 scallions (whites and greens separated) · 1 tbsp gochugaru · 1 tbsp gochujang · 1 tbsp soy sauce · 1 tbsp sesame oil · 2 cups water/stock 1. Heat sesame oil in a heavy pot (ttukbaegi if you have one). Render pork belly 3–4 min until golden. "Rendering the pork properly is what gives the broth its body — jjigae made with unrendered pork tastes thin no matter how much seasoning you add." 2. Add kimchi, onion, garlic — sauté in the rendered pork fat 3–4 min, letting the kimchi caramelize at the edges. "This blooms the fermented flavors and mellows raw sourness into something deeper." 3. Push mixture aside, toast gochugaru directly in the fat 15 sec, stir in gochujang + soy sauce, coat everything. 4. Pour 2 cups water + the 3 tbsp reserved kimchi brine ("liquid seasoning in its own right"). Boil, then simmer uncovered 15 min so the fat, gochujang and kimchi "fully marry into the broth." 5. Taste and adjust — more gochugaru/brine if the kimchi was young; leave it if tangy. 6. Nestle tofu + scallion whites, simmer 5–6 min, spooning broth over the tofu instead of stirring so the slabs stay intact. 7. Scallion greens on top, one final minute, serve bubbling with rice. "Best made with kimchi at least 3–4 weeks fermented — young kimchi lacks the sourness this stew is built around."
