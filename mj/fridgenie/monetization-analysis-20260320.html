Annual Plan Strategy

Offer both monthly and annual pricing:

  • Monthly: $4.99/month ($59.88/year)
  • Annual: $39.99/year ($3.33/month) — "Save 33%" badge
  • Annual plans reduce churn dramatically (annual churn ~20% vs monthly ~8%/month)
  • Upfront annual revenue helps with cash flow and AI cost forecasting
  • Show the monthly price crossed out with the annual savings prominently

Future Premium Features (Higher Tier Potential)

These could justify $7.99-9.99/mo or a separate "Chef" tier later:

  • AI Meal Planner — auto-generate weekly meal plans based on pantry, dietary needs, and budget. This alone could be a $5/mo feature.

Why AI costs stay LOW as we scale:

  • Fridgenie recipes are cached forever — generated once per dish, served to ALL users. 10K unique dishes = ~$1,000 one-time, then $0 ongoing.
  • Clean recipes are cached per URL — same recipe from AllRecipes.com is fetched once, served to every Pro user who views it.
  • Recipe suggestions use cheaper models (GPT-4.1-mini/nano) — ~$0.01 per search.
  • More users = better cache hit rate — at 50K users, 90%+ of recipe requests hit cache.

Key Insight: Margins IMPROVE with Scale

Because recipes are cached (generated once, served forever), AI costs grow much slower than revenue. At 50K users, 90%+ of requests hit cache. Your biggest cost is Apple's 15% cut, not AI. This is a very high-margin SaaS business once you pass ~500 Pro subscribers.

6. Pricing Recommendation

Final Recommendation

Launch Pricing (Now)

  • Monthly: $4.99/month
  • Annual: $39.99/year (Save 33%)
  • Free trial: 7-day Pro trial for new users

After 10K Users

  • Evaluate upgrading to $5.99/month
  • Or introduce "Chef" tier at $9.99/month
  • Grandfather early adopters at $4.99

Why $4.99 is right for NOW:

  1. Psychological pricing — "Under $5" is a proven sweet spot. $5.99 feels like $6, $4.99 feels like $4.
  2. Competitive positioning — matches Yummly, undercuts Mealime ($5.99), while offering more AI features.
  3. Value perception — unlimited AI recipes + clean recipe view + ad removal easily justifies $4.99. Users pay more for a single coffee.
  4. Growth phase pricing — you want USERS first, revenue second. Lower price = higher conversion = more data = better product = raise prices later.
  5. You can always raise prices — it's much harder to lower them. Start at $4.99, prove the value, then consider $5.99-7.99 for v3.0 with meal planning.

Why NOT to charge more right now:

  • The app is new with no reviews/ratings — users need social proof before paying premium
  • Feature set is strong but not yet "complete" (no meal planning, no cooking mode)
  • Higher price = higher expectations = more refunds if anything is buggy
  • Better to have 500 happy subscribers at $4.99 than 100 skeptical ones at $9.99
