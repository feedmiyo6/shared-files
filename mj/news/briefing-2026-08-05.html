Three stories this week all touch your world from different angles, even though none of them mention automotive Ethernet directly. Microchip now reports Thursday, one day later than the earlier estimate, with the stock already up 7% ahead of it — that's your read on demand sentiment heading in. Separately, the FCC is eyeing a ban on Chinese optical transceivers, the components that move data across datacenters and telecom backbones; if that goes through, it accelerates the same higher-speed-Ethernet build-out that's been lifting networking chipmakers all year, just from the trade-policy side instead of the technology side. And GE Appliances just picked Texas Instruments — not Microchip — for its next-gen connected microcontrollers, a reminder that the reshoring wave cuts both ways: good for domestic chipmakers broadly, but still a competitive fight for who wins each design win.
